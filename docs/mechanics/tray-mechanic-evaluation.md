# Should we introduce a TRAY mechanic? — honest evaluation

*game-designer + game-psychologist · "tray" = the 7-slot collection bar from the triple-match genre (Match Factory, Zen Match, Triple Tile, Tile Busters)*

## Verdict up front
A tray is **not an add-on — it's a genre pivot.** It replaces our sort/match-3 *core*, it doesn't augment it. BUT on the exact dimensions we keep saying we care about — manufactured tension→release, a real USP, and monetization — a **tray-collect core is very likely BETTER than another sort/match-3 clone.** Recommendation: **worth pivoting to, *if* we bring a twist** — and the Surge Lock is that twist.

## Why a tray is better on *our* scorecard
| Dimension | Flat sort / match-3 | Tray-collect | 
|---|---|---|
| **Built-in tension** | weak — you can always undo a bad sort | **strong — slots fill toward a hard fail; loss-aversion every tap** |
| **Release moment** | a rod completes (occasional) | **a triple clears + slots free up (constant micro-relief)** |
| **Fail-state monetization** | "stuck" (rare) | **"tray full" is frequent + deeply felt → +3 slots / undo / magnet = high-converting ad/IAP moments** |
| **Onboarding** | must learn sort rules | **tap-three, instantly understood (huge for CPI/IPM)** |
| **Market** | crowded | crowded *and* hot/high-ceiling (Match Factory tier) |

The tray's filling slots **are** a dopamine engine: every tap raises tension (am I cornering myself?), every triple is a release, and the near-full tray is pure Zeigarnik tension. That's the "manufacture joy" thing we've been chasing — and it's *intrinsic* to the mechanic, not bolted on.

## The catch (don't pivot blind)
- It's a **different game** from Cable Sort (sort/ball-sort family). Pivoting means parking the sort core, not improving it.
- The triple-match genre is **crowded and high-polish** — Match Factory set a brutal production bar. A bare clone loses. **A new entrant needs a real twist + premium feel.**

## The twist that justifies it: TRAY + SURGE LOCK + the Surge meter
Our two existing inventions port straight onto a tray and give it a USP:
- **Surge Lock in the pile:** some tiles are clamped — you **can't pick them up** until you **discharge** them by collecting a triple of an *adjacent* colour. Bursting a clamp frees the tile **and dumps charge into the Surge meter** → chain into a slow-mo MEGA SURGE. The lock becomes a *tray-management risk-and-reward* (do I spend slots near the clamp?).
- **Surge meter / combo:** fast consecutive triples charge it; popping it clears junk / grants a slot — our anticipation→release arc, now riding the tray's natural tension.

That combination — *tray tension + lock-as-reward + surge payoff* — is a defensible USP, not "Match Factory but worse."

## Self-QA
- **Assumptions:** tray tension out-performs sort for engagement/monetization (well-supported by genre performance); the Surge Lock reads on a tray (untested).
- **Confidence a tray core is a stronger commercial base than our sort core:** **~75%.**
- **Confidence we can *win* the crowded triple-match space:** **~35%** without standout polish + the twist; the twist is what we'd be betting on.
- **What would make this wrong:** the genre is past peak by the time we ship; our twist muddies the dead-simple tap-three clarity that makes the genre convert; we can't hit Match Factory's polish bar cheaply.
- **Cheapest kill-test (before any build):** a paper/click mock of tray + 1 lock, a 5-player "is this fun + do you get it in 10s?" test, and a CPI A/B of a tray+surge ad clip vs. the sort clip. Decide on data, not vibes.
